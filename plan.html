For RichardOctober 2026draft
The call

Run a 90-day pilot with one council. Residents report dumping in three taps, the council acts from an email, and our crews collect it the next working day and recycle it. If it works, every council that signs becomes a collection contract feeding our recycling.

The idea

A free app for residents, a console for councils and land managers, and Recycle Group crews and recycling behind it. Every report gets a public clock that runs until the rubbish is gone, then a receipt showing where it went. Reporting is the front door. Collection and recycling are the business.

Why it's a business

  • Councils already pay for this. Greater Geelong logged 6,619 requests to remove dumped rubbish in 2024-25, and more than 3,000 more by early 2026, at a cost to ratepayers of "hundreds of thousands of dollars each year". City of Greater Geelong Sourced
  • Nobody knows who to call. Councils handle household items on council land. EPA Victoria handles building waste, asbestos, garden waste, tyres, chemicals, packaging and old cars. Rail, main roads and parks each have their own owner. EPA Victoria Sourced
  • We sort that out automatically. The prototype finds the council from ABS boundaries and spots main roads and rail land from OpenStreetMap, then copies EPA Victoria by waste type.

What's already out there

Snap Send Solve sends reports to councils by email for free and charges councils for integration, duplicate filtering, analytics and messaging. Snap Send Solve Sourced Councils also run their own forms and phone lines, and EPA has a hotline.

None of them collect it, recycle it or show what happened to it. That's the gap, and it's what the group already does.

Prices aren't set. The nearest reference is Yarta's proposed council price of $1,999 a month for councils up to 50,000 residents. I'd pitch the console well under that and make the money on collections.

Why we win

  • We're the only one who can close the loop: report, collect next working day, recycle, and prove it with a receipt.
  • The public clock is the hook. Residents can see it moving, and fast councils get credit instead of complaints.
  • We already know prevention works. Making ten things free to drop off significantly cut illegal roadside dumping. We can offer free drop-off days to pilot councils. Need the before and after numbers
  • It scales past our crews. Rubbish.com.au's registered collectors could do collections in areas our crews don't cover. Proposed

What could go wrong

RiskHow we handle it
Councils see a conflict of interest: the reporting tool belongs to the collector.Routing is neutral and the council picks who collects. Reports by email are free whoever collects.
Councils don't want a public clock.The clock runs against their own targets, and every collection ends on good news. Start with a council that wants the credit.
"Public scrutiny of dumpers" turns into naming people.Scrutiny is on the dumping, not people. Regos and identities go only to the council and EPA, never on the site. Get legal advice before launch.
Fake or malicious reports.No cash for reports. Duplicates are merged. A photo and pin are needed before a crew is sent.
Hazardous material.Flagged at the report, EPA Victoria copied, never handled by a general crew.
Faces and number plates in public photos.Blur them automatically before photos go public.
Reports going to the wrong owner.One tap passes it on, the clock keeps running, and routing gets tuned per council.

The pilot

90 days in one area with a council we already work with. Proposed

  • Greater Geelong has the volume, and our crews and drop-off are local.
  • Hepburn Shire is smaller and already a client through the Clunes collection.
  • Hobsons Bay knows us from the collection booking tender.

Measure: median time to collect, share collected by the next working day, duplicates merged, kilograms recovered, and resident follow-up calls.

Decisions needed

  1. Name and domain: no.com.au. Confirm we hold it
  2. Pricing for the console and per collection.
  3. Which council to pilot with.
  4. Crew capacity for next-day collection in the pilot area.
  5. Legal review of the public clock, photos and regos.
  6. Whether collections run as Arrivy jobs, like the council booking build.
